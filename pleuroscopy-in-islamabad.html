What is Pleuroscopy?

Pleuroscopy, also called medical thoracoscopy, is a minimally invasive procedure used to directly examine the pleural space, which is the area between the lung and chest wall.

It may be considered when a patient has unexplained, recurrent or otherwise concerning pleural disease, particularly when information from imaging and pleural fluid testing is not sufficient to establish a diagnosis.

Depending on the clinical situation, pleuroscopy may allow direct visualization of the pleura and targeted biopsy. Some therapeutic procedures may also be performed during the same procedure when appropriate.

When May Pleuroscopy Be Considered?

Recurrent Pleural Effusion

Pleuroscopy may be considered when pleural fluid continues to accumulate and the underlying cause remains uncertain after initial evaluation.

Suspected Pleural Disease

It may help investigate suspected pleural tuberculosis, malignancy or other diseases affecting the pleural lining.

Negative Fluid Cytology

When pleural fluid testing does not establish a diagnosis, targeted pleural biopsy may sometimes provide additional diagnostic information.

Pleural Abnormalities on Imaging

Pleural thickening, nodularity or other abnormalities seen on CT or ultrasound may require further evaluation depending on the clinical context.

How Pleuroscopy Is Performed

  1. Clinical assessment: The patient's symptoms, imaging and previous test results are reviewed to determine whether pleuroscopy is appropriate.
  2. Imaging and procedural planning: Chest imaging and, when appropriate, ultrasound may be used to help identify a suitable and safe access site.
  3. Local anesthesia and sedation: Pleuroscopy is commonly performed with local anesthesia and appropriate sedation. The exact approach depends on the patient and procedure.
  4. Small access site: A small opening is made in the chest wall to allow the thoracoscope to enter the pleural space.
  5. Pleural examination: The pleural surfaces are examined directly and targeted biopsies may be obtained when required.
  6. Additional treatment when appropriate: Pleural fluid drainage and selected therapeutic procedures, such as pleurodesis, may be considered depending on the underlying condition.
  7. Observation after the procedure: The patient is monitored after the procedure. Discharge timing depends on the procedure performed and the patient's clinical condition.

Potential Benefits of Pleuroscopy

  • Direct visualization of the pleural space.
  • Ability to obtain targeted pleural biopsies when indicated.
  • May help establish the cause of unexplained or recurrent pleural effusion.
  • Can provide additional information when fluid testing and imaging are inconclusive.
  • Selected therapeutic procedures may be possible during the same procedure.

Whether pleuroscopy is appropriate depends on the patient's medical history, imaging findings, previous investigations and overall clinical condition.

Pleuroscopy for Pleural Effusion

Pleural effusion has many possible causes, including infection, tuberculosis, malignancy, heart or kidney disease and other conditions. The evaluation usually begins with clinical assessment, imaging and analysis of pleural fluid when appropriate.

If the cause remains uncertain, or if there is concern about pleural disease, a specialist may consider additional investigation such as pleural biopsy or medical thoracoscopy.

Pleuroscopy is therefore not required for every patient with pleural effusion. The decision is individualized according to the clinical findings.

Pleuroscopy and Pleural Biopsy

One of the important uses of medical thoracoscopy is obtaining tissue from an abnormal area of the pleura. A targeted biopsy can provide tissue for laboratory and pathological examination when a definitive diagnosis cannot be established through less invasive tests.

The need for biopsy, the number of samples and the laboratory tests required depend on the suspected diagnosis.

Safety and Recovery

As with any medical procedure, pleuroscopy has potential risks. These can include bleeding, infection, pain, air leak or pneumothorax and complications related to sedation or anesthesia.

The medical team assesses the patient's health, medications, imaging and other relevant factors before the procedure. Recovery and observation requirements vary from patient to patient.

Patients should discuss the expected benefits, risks, alternatives and preparation instructions with their treating specialist before undergoing the procedure.
