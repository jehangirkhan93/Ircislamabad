What Are Pulmonary Function Tests?

Pulmonary function tests are a group of non-invasive breathing tests that provide objective measurements of lung function. Common measurements include airflow, lung volumes, the speed at which you can exhale, and markers of airway inflammation.

At IRC Islamabad, pulmonary function testing is performed in a specialist pulmonary clinic setting with clinical interpretation by a consultant pulmonologist rather than as a standalone laboratory report.

Important: A normal PFT does not always exclude respiratory disease, because conditions such as asthma can be variable. Repeat testing, peak-flow monitoring or additional investigations may be appropriate when symptoms persist.

Who May Need Pulmonary Function Testing?

  • Recurrent cough, wheezing, chest tightness or shortness of breath - see Asthma in Islamabad guide
  • Symptoms that vary - worse at night, early morning, during exercise, or after exposure to pollen, dust or smoke
  • History of asthma, COPD, bronchiectasis requiring monitoring - Asthma Management
  • Evaluation of breathlessness that returns or does not improve
  • Assessment before starting or adjusting inhaled treatment - Inhalers for Asthma
  • Monitoring response to asthma or COPD treatment
  • Evaluation of smoking-related lung disease - COPD Treatment
  • Occupational dust exposure - Spirometry Test Guide

Pulmonary Function Tests Performed at IRC Islamabad

1. Spirometry (Gold-Standard PFT)

Measures airflow and lung volumes including FEV1, FVC and FEV1/FVC ratio. Can be performed before and after bronchodilator to assess reversibility. Essential for asthma and COPD assessment.

Learn about Spirometry →

2. FeNO Testing

Fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO) measures Type 2 airway inflammation. A painless 10-second breath test. Useful in asthma phenotyping and monitoring. See Asthma in Islamabad.

3. Lung Age Estimation

Some spirometers estimate lung age based on FEV1 and demographic data. Educational measure to understand smoking-related lung changes. Related: COPD Treatment

4. Exhaled CO Monitoring

Exhaled carbon monoxide test - helpful in smoking cessation counselling. Related to Spirometry and COPD.

5. Peak Flow Assessment

Peak expiratory flow (PEF) measures how fast you can exhale. Useful for monitoring asthma variability. See Asthma Management in Islamabad.

6. Bronchodilator Reversibility

Measures change in airflow after inhaled bronchodilator. An improvement may support variable airflow limitation seen in asthma. Learn more: Inhalers Guide

Spirometry - What It Measures

Spirometry is the most important pulmonary function test. You take a deep breath and blow out as hard and fast as possible into a mouthpiece connected to a spirometer. Watch video above where Dr Jehangir explains spirometer device.

  • FEV1: Forced expiratory volume in 1 second
  • FVC: Forced vital capacity
  • FEV1/FVC ratio: Proportion - reduced in obstructive diseases
  • Flow-volume loop: Graphical representation

For suspected asthma, spirometry is often performed before and after a bronchodilator (salbutamol). An improvement in FEV1 may support reversible airflow limitation. Details: Spirometry Test Cost & Procedure

FeNO Test - What It Can Tell You

FeNO stands for fractional exhaled nitric oxide. It is a non-invasive breath test performed by exhaling steadily into a FeNO device (NObreath) for about 10 seconds - shown in video above.

A higher FeNO may support Type 2 airway inflammation, common in allergic asthma. However, FeNO is not exclusive to asthma and a normal FeNO does not exclude asthma.

FeNO LevelInterpretationClinical Context
<25 ppb (adults)Lower - less Type 2 inflammationAsthma not excluded
25-50 ppbIntermediateInterpret with symptoms, treatment history, eosinophils, IgE
>50 ppbHigher - supports Type 2 inflammationMay support allergic/eosinophilic asthma

FeNO is a supporting test, not a stand-alone diagnosis. Results are interpreted alongside symptoms, spirometry, treatment history, blood eosinophils and IgE. Learn more in Asthma in Islamabad - Diagnosis.

Lung Age & Exhaled CO Monitoring

Lung Age

Some spirometry devices calculate an estimated lung age based on your FEV1 compared to predicted values. If your lung age is higher than your chronological age, it may indicate accelerated lung function loss, often related to smoking, vape or dust exposure - as explained in video by Dr Jehangir.

Exhaled CO

Exhaled carbon monoxide monitoring measures CO in your breath. Useful for smoking cessation counselling and assessing recent smoke exposure.

Peak Flow Monitoring

Peak expiratory flow (PEF) is the maximum speed you can blow air out. It can be measured in clinic or at home with a peak flow meter. As Dr Jehangir mentions in video, when all tests are normal but asthma suspicion remains, we advise Peak Flow Diary.

  • Diurnal variability - lower in morning, higher in evening
  • Drop during pollen season, infection or trigger exposure - see Pollen Allergy Islamabad
  • Improvement after bronchodilator or controller treatment - Inhalers Guide

How to Prepare for PFTs at IRC

Before Your Test

  • Follow IRC's specific instructions - preparation depends on tests planned
  • Avoid smoking for at least 6 hours if possible, and vigorous exercise, heavy meals and alcohol for 4 hours before spirometry
  • Avoid tight clothing that restricts breathing
  • Inform clinic about recent chest infection, chest pain, recent surgery, or use of inhalers
  • Bring list of medicines, previous spirometry reports, chest X-ray or CT if available
  • For FeNO, avoid nitrate-rich foods, smoking and exercise before test where possible

How Are PFTs Interpreted?

  • Normal: FEV1, FVC and FEV1/FVC within predicted range - does not exclude asthma if variable - How Asthma is Diagnosed
  • Obstructive pattern: Reduced FEV1/FVC ratio - seen in asthma, COPD, bronchiectasis
  • Restrictive pattern: Reduced FVC with normal or high FEV1/FVC - may suggest interstitial disease, chest wall disease, obesity - requires further evaluation
  • Reversible obstruction: Improvement after bronchodilator supports asthma

Frequently Asked Questions

What pulmonary function tests are available at IRC Islamabad?

Depending on clinical need, IRC offers respiratory assessment that can include spirometry, FeNO, lung age estimation, exhaled CO monitoring and peak-flow assessment. Appropriate tests are selected according to symptoms and evaluation. Watch video above for device explanation.

What is spirometry?

Spirometry measures airflow and lung volumes such as FEV1, FVC and FEV1/FVC. It is gold-standard for assessing airflow limitation and helps evaluate asthma and COPD. See Spirometry Guide.

What is FeNO test?

FeNO measures fractional exhaled nitric oxide and provides information about Type 2 airway inflammation. It can support asthma assessment but should be interpreted together with symptoms and other findings. Related: Asthma in Islamabad.

How should I prepare for PFTs?

Preparation depends on the test. IRC may advise avoiding smoking, vigorous exercise, heavy meals or certain inhalers before testing. Follow instructions provided at booking.

Does a normal spirometry exclude asthma?

No. Asthma is variable and spirometry can be normal between episodes. If symptoms are suggestive, repeat testing, peak-flow monitoring or other investigations may be considered. As Dr Jehangir mentions, when all tests are normal but suspicion remains, we advise Peak Flow Diary.
