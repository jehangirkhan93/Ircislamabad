1. Introduction

Lipoid pneumonia is an uncommon inflammatory lung condition where lipids accumulate in alveoli. Exogenous lipoid pneumonia has been reported after inhalation of oil-based substances, including mineral oils and components of vape liquids. Vaping-associated lung injury (EVALI) emerged as a clinical entity with presentations ranging from ground glass opacities to consolidations.

We present a 29-year-old male vape user evaluated at Islamabad Respiratory Clinic, G-11 Markaz, Islamabad, with cough, chest pain and shortness of breath, with imaging showing right middle lobe pneumonia and possible lipoid pneumonia, with subsequent clinical improvement.

2. Case Presentation

A 29-year-old male, known vape user, presented to IRC Islamabad with:

  • Cough for several days
  • Chest pain, localized
  • Shortness of breath on exertion
  • No high-grade fever reported at presentation, no hemoptysis
  • Past history: regular vaping use, no known asthma, no tuberculosis contact

On examination, respiratory system examination revealed reduced breath sounds and crackles in right lower and middle zones. Systemic examination was unremarkable. Oxygen saturation was monitored and routine labs were obtained.

5. Bronchoscopy and Laboratory Findings

Bronchoscopy was performed as per indication to evaluate persistent consolidation and to obtain bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) for analysis.

  • Bronchoscopy: airway examination noted and BAL obtained from affected lobe
  • BAL cytology: foamy lipid-laden macrophages observed, which can be seen in lipid-associated inflammatory processes
  • Microbiology: bacterial cultures, fungal and tuberculosis workup performed to exclude alternative causes
  • No malignancy seen on cytology

Foamy macrophages can be observed in various conditions and are not diagnostic alone. Diagnosis of lipoid pneumonia requires clinical, radiologic and pathologic correlation and exclusion of infection.

6. Diagnosis

Provisional assessment: Right middle lobe pneumonia with possible vaping-associated lung injury and possible lipoid pneumonia in a 29-year-old vape user. Differential includes community-acquired pneumonia, aspiration pneumonia, organizing pneumonia and EVALI. Final diagnosis was made after clinical correlation and exclusion of alternative causes.

7. Management and Clinical Outcome

Management included:

  • Counseling for complete vaping cessation and avoidance of oily inhalants
  • Supportive care, hydration and symptomatic treatment
  • Antibiotic therapy as per pneumonia protocol while awaiting cultures
  • Monitoring of oxygenation and clinical parameters
  • Follow-up imaging and clinic review

Clinical Outcome

At one-month follow-up, the patient was clinically well. Chest examination was clear, and follow-up chest X-ray was reported as normal. This documented clinical and radiological improvement was reassuring. Long-term follow-up and smoking and vaping cessation counseling were advised.

8. Discussion: Lipoid Pneumonia and Vaping

Exogenous lipoid pneumonia results from aspiration or inhalation of lipid-containing substances. Vape liquids contain propylene glycol, vegetable glycerin, flavorings and other additives that may include lipids. When aerosolized and inhaled repeatedly, these can cause inflammatory response in distal airways and alveoli.

HRCT findings in lipoid pneumonia typically include:

  • Low attenuation consolidations (fat density -30 to -150 HU may be seen but not always)
  • Ground glass opacities
  • Predilection for dependent lobes, right middle lobe and lower lobes
  • Crazy-paving pattern in some cases

EVALI and lipoid pneumonia overlap clinically. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) has reported cases of vaping-associated lung injury requiring hospitalization. Vaping cessation is cornerstone of management.

9. Prevention and When to Seek Care in Islamabad

  • Avoid vaping and e-cigarettes, especially oil-based or unregulated products
  • Seek pulmonology evaluation if you develop cough, chest pain, shortness of breath within weeks of vaping
  • Do not self-medicate with steroids or antibiotics without medical evaluation

10. Frequently Asked Questions

What is lipoid pneumonia?

Lipoid pneumonia is inflammation due to accumulation of lipid material in alveoli. It can be exogenous from inhaled oils or endogenous.

Can vaping cause lipoid pneumonia?

Cases have been reported where vaping was associated with lipoid pneumonia pattern. Risk appears related to constituents of vape liquid and frequency of use. Not all vape users develop lung injury.

How is it diagnosed?

Diagnosis needs combination of exposure history, imaging (HRCT chest), bronchoscopy with lavage showing lipid-laden macrophages, and exclusion of infection and other causes.

Is it reversible?

Many patients show clinical and radiological improvement after cessation of exposure and supportive care, as seen in this case with normal follow-up X-ray at one month. Long-term follow-up is still advised.
