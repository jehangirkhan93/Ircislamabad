Pulmonary Function Tests (PFTs) are a group of non-invasive breathing tests that show how well your lungs and airways are working. At Islamabad Respiratory Clinic (IRC), G-11 Markaz, inside Saeed International Hospital, PFTs are performed as part of specialist pulmonology assessment by Dr Muhammad Israr and Dr Jehangir Khan, as shown in the video above.

What PFTs Are Done at IRC Islamabad?

  • Spirometry: Measures FEV1, FVC, FEV1/FVC ratio — gold standard for asthma & COPD
  • FeNO (NObreath): Fractional exhaled nitric oxide — Type 2 airway inflammation
  • Lung Age: Estimated lung age vs chronological age — smoking counselling
  • CO Monitor: Exhaled carbon monoxide — smoking cessation monitoring
  • Peak Flow: Peak expiratory flow diary for variable asthma — as Dr Jehangir advises when spirometry normal

Who Needs PFTs?

If you have chronic cough, wheeze, shortness of breath, chest tightness, or known asthma or COPD, PFTs help assess severity and response to treatment. Also relevant for pollen allergy, allergy and unexplained breathlessness. Related: Inhaler technique guide.

How to Prepare

  • Avoid smoking 1 hour, vigorous exercise 30 min, heavy meal 2 hours before
  • Avoid alcohol 4 hours before, tight clothing that restricts breathing
  • Continue medicines unless advised — bring list of inhalers
  • Bring previous PFTs / chest X-ray / asthma action plan

Frequently Asked Questions

What pulmonary function tests are available at IRC Islamabad?

Depending on clinical need, IRC offers respiratory assessment that can include spirometry, FeNO, lung age estimation, exhaled CO monitoring and peak-flow assessment. Appropriate tests are selected according to symptoms and evaluation. Watch video above for device explanation.

What is spirometry?

Spirometry measures airflow and lung volumes such as FEV1, FVC and FEV1/FVC. It is gold-standard for assessing airflow limitation and helps evaluate asthma and COPD. See Spirometry Guide.

What is FeNO test?

FeNO measures fractional exhaled nitric oxide and provides information about Type 2 airway inflammation. It can support asthma assessment but should be interpreted together with symptoms and other findings. Related: Asthma in Islamabad.

How should I prepare for PFTs?

Preparation depends on the test. IRC may advise avoiding smoking, vigorous exercise, heavy meals or certain inhalers before testing. Follow instructions provided at booking.

Does a normal spirometry exclude asthma?

No. Asthma is variable and spirometry can be normal between episodes. If symptoms are suggestive, repeat testing, peak-flow monitoring or other investigations may be considered. As Dr Jehangir mentions, when all tests are normal but suspicion remains, we advise Peak Flow Diary.
