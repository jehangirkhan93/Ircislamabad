Asthma in Islamabad: Why It's Rising, and How We Diagnose It

By Islamabad Respiratory Clinic (IRC), G-11 Markaz, Islamabad · Medically reviewed by Dr Muhammad Israr & Dr Jehangir Khan, Consultant Respiratory Physicians

If you find yourself wheezing during the winter smog, waking up short of breath after Islamabad's spring pollen season, or reaching for an inhaler every time the weather turns, you are not alone. Asthma cases in Islamabad have been climbing steadily over the past decade, and local doctors increasingly link this to the city's unique mix of air pollution, allergenic trees, and seasonal weather swings. This guide explains the main causes of asthma in Islamabad and walks you through the diagnostic tests available at IRC to get a precise, personalised diagnosis.

Why Is Asthma So Common in Islamabad?

Asthma is a chronic inflammatory condition of the airways that reacts to triggers in the environment. Islamabad, despite its reputation as a green city, has several conditions that make it a hotspot for asthma and allergic airway disease.

1. Seasonal Smog and Vehicular Pollution

Every autumn and winter, Islamabad and the wider Rawalpindi-Islamabad belt experience thick smog caused by vehicle emissions, dust, and crop residue burning drifting in from Punjab. Fine particulate matter (PM2.5) settles deep into the lungs, irritating the airway lining and triggering asthma flare-ups, especially in children and the elderly.

2. Paper Mulberry and Pollen Allergy

Islamabad has one of the highest concentrations of Paper Mulberry (Broussonetia papyrifera) trees of any city in the region. Every spring, typically from March through April, these trees release enormous quantities of airborne pollen. For sensitised individuals, this pollen surge is one of the single biggest triggers of allergic rhinitis and asthma exacerbations seen in the city each year.

3. Construction and Road Dust

Islamabad's constant expansion means ongoing construction, unpaved shoulders, and frequent road works. This generates fine dust that, combined with vehicle exhaust, worsens airway inflammation for anyone with sensitive or already-diagnosed asthmatic lungs.

4. Biomass and Waste Burning

Open burning of garden waste, garbage, and biomass fuel in and around residential sectors remains common. Smoke from these fires contains irritant gases and soot particles that are well documented triggers for asthma and chronic bronchitis.

5. Sudden Weather Changes

Islamabad's climate shifts quickly between hot, humid summers, monsoon spells, and cold winters. Rapid temperature and humidity swings are a recognised trigger for airway hyper-reactivity, and many patients report their symptoms flare specifically during these transitional weeks.

6. Indoor Allergens

Dust mites, mould in damp, poorly ventilated homes, and pet dander add an indoor layer of triggers on top of outdoor pollution, particularly during Islamabad's humid monsoon months.

7. Genetics and Family History

As anywhere else, a family history of asthma, eczema, or allergic rhinitis increases individual risk. In Islamabad, this genetic predisposition often combines with the environmental triggers above to produce more frequent and more severe symptoms.

How Asthma Is Diagnosed at IRC

Asthma can be under-diagnosed or confused with other conditions such as allergic rhinitis, COPD, or a simple chest infection. At Islamabad Respiratory Clinic, our consultant pulmonologists combine a detailed clinical history with objective lung-function and allergy testing to reach an accurate diagnosis and build a treatment plan tailored to your specific triggers.

Spirometry

The gold-standard lung function test. You breathe forcefully into a sensor that measures how much air you can exhale and how fast. At IRC, spirometry is performed with a bronchodilator reversibility test, which helps confirm whether narrowed airways are due to asthma and how well they respond to treatment.

FeNO Testing (Fractional Exhaled Nitric Oxide)

A quick, painless breath test that measures nitric oxide levels in your exhaled breath, a direct marker of airway inflammation. FeNO helps confirm an asthma diagnosis, predicts how well a patient will respond to inhaled steroids, and is used to fine-tune treatment over time.

Allergy (Skin Prick) Testing

Identifies the exact allergens driving your symptoms - whether that's Paper Mulberry pollen, dust mites, mould, or pet dander. Pinpointing your personal triggers allows us to give targeted avoidance advice alongside medical treatment.

Serum IgE (Total & Specific)

A blood test that measures Immunoglobulin E, the antibody responsible for allergic reactions. Elevated total IgE supports an allergic asthma diagnosis, while specific IgE panels can confirm sensitivity to individual allergens.

Blood Eosinophil Count

Eosinophils are white blood cells linked to allergic inflammation. A raised eosinophil count on a simple blood test helps classify your asthma as "eosinophilic," which guides the choice of inhaled or, in select cases, biologic therapy.

Peak Flow & Chest Imaging

Peak flow monitoring tracks day-to-day variation in airway narrowing, while a chest X-ray helps rule out other causes of breathlessness such as infection or structural lung disease when clinically indicated.

Why get tested rather than just guessing? Asthma, allergic rhinitis, and even anxiety-related breathlessness can look similar on the surface. Objective tests like Spirometry and FeNO remove the guesswork, confirm the diagnosis, and let your doctor choose the right inhaler, dose, and add-on therapy from the very first visit - reducing flare-ups and emergency room visits over time.

Managing Asthma in an Islamabad Climate

Alongside prescribed inhaler therapy, patients in Islamabad benefit from a few practical, city-specific habits:

  • Check the daily Air Quality Index (AQI) before outdoor exercise, especially between November and February.
  • Keep windows closed and use an air purifier at home during the March-April pollen season.
  • Avoid outdoor activity during and immediately after biomass or waste burning nearby.
  • Wash bedding weekly in hot water to reduce dust mite exposure, particularly during the humid monsoon.
  • Always carry your reliever inhaler when travelling around the city, especially during smog season.
  • Never stop or reduce your controller inhaler without medical advice, even when you feel well.
