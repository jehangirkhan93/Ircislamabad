Pulmonary Function Tests Performed at IRC Islamabad

By Dr Jehangir Khan & Dr Muhammad Israr | IRC Clinic, Saeed International Hospital, G-11 Markaz Islamabad | 0343-1510025 | Updated 29 Sep 2026

Pulmonary Function Tests (PFTs) are breathing tests used to assess how well the lungs and airways are working. At IRC Islamabad, respiratory assessment may include spirometry, FeNO and peak-flow evaluation depending on the patient's symptoms and clinical needs.

Test results are interpreted together with the patient's medical history, symptoms and examination. A pulmonary function test can help clinicians investigate conditions such as asthma, COPD and other causes of breathing difficulty.

📁

Respiratory and Asthma Assessment at IRC Islamabad

A structured assessment can help identify the cause of breathing symptoms and guide treatment.

Depending on the clinical situation, assessment may include several complementary tests and reviews.

  • 1. Spirometry: Measures airflow and values such as FEV1 and FVC. It can help assess airflow limitation.
  • 2. FeNO: Measures fractional exhaled nitric oxide and can provide information about type 2 airway inflammation.
  • 3. Allergy assessment: Allergy testing may be considered when an allergic trigger is suspected.
  • 4. Inhaler technique review: Correct inhaler technique can be checked when inhaled medication is prescribed.
  • 5. Asthma action planning: Patients with asthma may receive an individualized plan for monitoring symptoms and responding to worsening symptoms.

For patients with severe asthma, treatment decisions may consider symptoms, exacerbation history, blood eosinophils, IgE, allergy status, FeNO and other clinical factors. Biologic treatment is considered individually when appropriate.

Pulmonary Function Tests Performed at IRC Islamabad

1. Spirometry (PFT)

Measures airflow and commonly reports FEV1, FVC and the FEV1/FVC ratio. Results are interpreted in clinical context.

2. FeNO Test

A non-invasive test that provides information about type 2 airway inflammation and may support asthma assessment.

3. Lung Age Assessment

Some lung-function devices provide an estimated lung-age measure based on breathing-test results. It is not a diagnosis.

4. Carbon Monoxide Assessment

Exhaled carbon monoxide testing can be used as part of smoking-related respiratory assessment and smoking-cessation support.

5. Peak Flow Monitoring

Peak-flow measurements can help some patients monitor changes in airflow over time, particularly when asthma is suspected.

6. Individualized Testing

The appropriate combination of tests depends on symptoms, medical history and the clinician's assessment.

FeNO Test - What It Can Tell You

FeNO (fractional exhaled nitric oxide) is a non-invasive breathing test that can provide information about type 2 airway inflammation. It may be useful when evaluating asthma and when assessing response to anti-inflammatory treatment.

FeNO should not be interpreted by itself. Symptoms, medication history, spirometry and other clinical findings can affect how the result is interpreted.

FeNO ResultGeneral InterpretationClinical Context
<25 ppbLower FeNO levelLess evidence of type 2 airway inflammation, although asthma is not excluded.
25-50 ppbIntermediate levelInterpretation should consider symptoms, treatment history and other findings.
>50 ppbHigher FeNO levelMay support the presence of type 2 airway inflammation; clinical context remains important.

FeNO and Assessment of Severe Asthma

For patients with severe asthma, FeNO can be one part of a broader assessment. Depending on the clinical picture, a specialist may also consider blood eosinophils, IgE, allergy status, exacerbation history, current treatment and other clinical factors.

  • Omalizumab: May be considered for eligible patients with allergic asthma.
  • Dupilumab: May be considered for eligible patients with appropriate type 2 inflammatory features.
  • Mepolizumab or other anti-IL-5/IL-5R therapies: May be considered for eligible patients with eosinophilic asthma.

The choice of biologic treatment is individualized and should be made by the treating specialist after reviewing the patient's clinical history, test results and eligibility criteria.
